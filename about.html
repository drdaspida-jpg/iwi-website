Imperial Women Initiative — Organizational Profile

Who We Are

Imperial Women Initiative (IWI)

A women-led, non-governmental and non-religious organization committed to inclusive and sustainable community development.

Imperial Women Initiative (IWI) was conceived in 2024 after its founder piloted a grassroots project that expanded access to violence-free spaces for women and girls in rural communities of Bauchi State. The project convened about 80 dedicated stakeholders — traditional leaders, government ministries and agencies, health workers, civil society organizations and youth advocates — who shared a common passion: ensuring that every woman and girl can live safely and reach her full potential.

Building on the project's success, IWI became a fully registered, independent non-governmental organization in 2025. IWI's early community impact activities — setting up safe circles, training peer mentors and linking survivors to services — demonstrated that empowerment and dignity grow when violence is eliminated.

These experiences inspired the organization to broaden its mandate from protection alone to an integrated model combining social services, economic empowerment, health and education. Although headquartered in Bauchi, IWI designs programmes ready for scale across northern Nigeria and, ultimately, other regions of the country.

Grounded in community partnership and driven by evidence, IWI is positioning itself as a catalyst for nationwide and global change — building safer, stronger and more equitable communities for all.

Vision

A flourishing and empowered society

A flourishing and empowered society where women, girls, children, and other vulnerable groups enjoy equal access to opportunities, resources, and rights — championed by supportive male advocates — for inclusive and sustainable development.

Mission

Strengthening community development

To strengthen community development by delivering impactful programs that empower women, girls, children, men, and other vulnerable groups through economic empowerment, access to health care, education, and social support — while actively promoting violence-free spaces for all.

Governance

A clear structure for accountable leadership

IWI has a formal organizational structure with defined roles and responsibilities.

Board of Directors

The Board provides direct supervision over management on programme direction, resource mobilization and allocation, policy formulation and advocacy in project locations. It brings together development experts, media, law and private consultants.

Executive Director

The Executive Director is accountable to the Board and coordinates, manages, supervises and supports the work of all staff. Victoria B. Ayodele serves as Founder and Executive Director.

Management Team

The ED and four managers make up the management team: Programme Manager, Finance/Administrative Manager, Human Resource Manager and M&E Manager.

Operational Reporting

Programme officers and trainers report to the Programme Manager. Logistics, gender, communication, fundraising officers, driver, security and office assistant report to the Finance and Admin Manager.

The Programme Manager coordinates programme activities. The Finance and Administrative Manager handles financial transactions, internal controls and administration. The Human Resource Manager oversees personnel matters including compensation, hiring, performance management and training. The M&E Manager is responsible for monitoring and evaluation and operational research. All four managers report to the Executive Director.

Our Goal

Inclusive and sustainable community development

To promote inclusive and sustainable community development by empowering individuals through effective programs, capacity building, policy advocacy, and research, with a strong focus on gender equity, food security, and resilience.

Strategic Objectives

Five priorities that drive the goal

01

Program Implementation and Project Development

Design and implement high-impact programs addressing social, economic, health and educational needs; monitor and evaluate outcomes to ensure effectiveness and sustainability.

02

Sustainable Empowerment for Food Security and Economic Stabilization

Promote women’s and youth entrepreneurship through skills training and livelihood support, while enhancing resilience through climate-smart agriculture and sustainable food systems.

03

Capacity Building, Counseling, and Training

Build individual and organizational capacities in leadership, vocational skills, mental health and life skills, with tailored counseling and training for holistic development.

04

Policy Advocacy and Influencing for Women’s Development

Advocate for gender-sensitive policies and legal frameworks, and engage government, stakeholders and civil society to drive systemic change.

05

Research, Learning, and Knowledge Sharing

Conduct participatory research and impact assessments and disseminate success stories, best practices and learning through reports, conferences and digital platforms.
